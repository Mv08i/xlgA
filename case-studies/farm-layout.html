🏡 Farm Layout Cases

A good farm layout makes daily operations twice as effective. This guide provides four styles of layout schemes—compact, automated, aesthetic, and production—each with a different focus, for you to choose as a reference based on your playstyle. Layout isn't a one-time decision but a dynamic process that evolves with the game's stages—compact start in year one, transition to automation in year two, then evolve toward aesthetics or extreme production in year three. Understanding the design principles behind each layout matters more than memorizing specific coordinates.

💡 Layout Principles
  • Proximity principle: Place high-frequency buildings (shipping bin, processing equipment) near the doorstep. The shorter the walking distance, the more time you save each day
  • Zoned management: Clearly separate the planting zone, animal zone, processing zone, and decoration zone, to avoid functional crossover and confusion
  • Path planning: Leave wide aisles to avoid detours wasting time. Main paths should be 2 tiles wide for horseback; branch paths 1 tile is enough
  • Reserve expansion space: Leave room for future upgrades (keg clusters, greenhouse, island farm) to avoid repeated demolition
  • Sprinkler alignment: Divide the planting zone neatly according to sprinkler coverage (3×3 or 5×5) to maximize space utilization
  • Visual guidance: Use paths, fences, and flower beds to guide the eye, making the farm look orderly rather than cluttered

✅ Space Utilization Advice

Spacious farms (Standard, Wilderness) can accommodate multiple layout elements at once; tight-space farms (Hilltop, Riverland) require strict functional zoning, with every inch of land having a clear purpose. Beginners should start with the Standard Farm, to avoid stacking space pressure on top of learning difficulty.

🌱 Scheme One: Compact Layout (Beginner Recommended)

The core idea is to concentrate all facilities near the spawn point, minimizing walking distance—suited for the first year when funds and materials are limited.

Layout Points

  • 3×3 shipping bin + storage chests + processing equipment at the doorstep
  • 5×5 planting zone just south of the house (with basic sprinklers)
  • Build a basic coop on the east side, convenient for collecting eggs in the morning
  • Reserve a small foraging / fruit tree area on the west side
  • All zones reachable within a 10-second walk
Pros Cons
Small footprint, easy to build, very low movement cost Limited capacity, hard to expand at scale
Beginner-friendly, fast payback early Needs migration and reorganization in mid-late game
✅ Suited For

First-year beginners, casual players, people who don't want to spend too much time planning.

⚙️ Scheme Two: Automated Layout (Efficiency Build)

The core idea is to use sprinklers, auto-feeders, and processing lines to minimize manual operation, letting the farm "run itself"—suited for players who pursue passive income.

Layout Points

  • Lay the planting zone in sheets according to the "quality sprinkler 5×5 range," neatly arranged
  • Only build deluxe coops/barns in the animal zone (with auto-feeders)
  • Concentrate the processing zone: keg matrix + mayonnaise machine + cheese press
  • Processing zone adjacent to the shipping bin, one-click shipping
  • Place silos at the animal zone entrance, convenient for replenishing hay
  • Reserve large open space for future keg expansion
Pros Cons
Minimal daily operation, high passive income Large early investment (materials, gold)
Scale can expand infinitely Layout is rigid, lacks aesthetics
✅ Suited For

Players pursuing efficiency and idle income; the main layout from year two onward.

🎨 Scheme Three: Aesthetic Layout (Scenery Build)

The core idea is to prioritize visual effect, using paths, fences, flower beds, and decorative buildings to create a "pleasing to the eye" manor.

Layout Points

  • Lay main paths with stone / wood planks, connecting all functional zones
  • Frame the planting zone with fences into a garden-style grid
  • Place sculptures, fountains, and benches in a central plaza
  • Plant fruit trees symmetrically along both sides of the path, forming a tree-lined avenue
  • Pair the animal zone with ponds and lawns to mimic ranch scenery
  • Plant flowers around the house, swapping color schemes seasonally
Pros Cons
Visual enjoyment, perfect for screenshots Takes up lots of space, lower capacity
Strong sense of achievement, suited for slow-paced players Decorative material collection is time-consuming
✅ Suited For

Scenery lovers, casual players, players who enjoy decoration and design.

🏭 Scheme Four: Production Layout (Extreme Build)

The core idea is to squeeze every inch of land's productive capacity—large-scale planting + animal husbandry + processing, pursuing gold maximization.

Layout Points

  • Planting zone occupies 60% of the farm area, fully covered by sprinkler grid
  • Greenhouse fully planted with Ancient Fruit (wine ingredient)
  • Multiple deluxe barn clusters, 12 pigs per full pen
  • Keg zone in the house cellar (Robin expansion), surface reserved for planting
  • Storage chest matrix sorted by material / product / gift
  • Desert farm or island farm as supplementary capacity
Pros Cons
Extreme gold output Heavy daily operation load
Strong sense of scale, achievement explosion Requires lots of maintenance time
✅ Suited For

Veteran players pursuing extreme profit with ample game time.

🛠️ General Layout Tips

📦 Storage chest sorting

Sort chests by "materials / crops / animal products / gifts / ore," distinguishing them by color or labels.

🚪 Shipping bin nearby

Place the shipping bin in the center of the processing zone; drop processed goods straight in, saving transport time.

🛤️ Widen main paths

Main paths at least 2 tiles wide, so riding a horse doesn't stutter; branch paths 1 tile is enough, saving space.

🌿 Use fences wisely

Fences both decorate and guide paths; hardwood fences are the most durable and the first choice for long-term layouts.

💡 Layout Evolution Advice

Don't chase a perfect layout from the start. Start with compact in year one, transition to automated in year two, then evolve toward aesthetic or extreme production in year three. The farm is alive and will adjust with your needs.

🛤️ Path and Walkway Strategy

Paths aren't just decoration—they're a key factor affecting daily efficiency. Good path planning can save you hundreds of steps a day, accumulating significant time savings over the long run:

  • Main paths 2 tiles wide: Paths connecting the house, shipping bin, and processing zone should be at least 2 tiles wide so riding a horse doesn't stutter. Lay wooden or stone paths to boost speed.
  • Branch paths 1 tile wide: Inner paths in the planting zone can be 1 tile, saving space. Use wooden paths or simple trodden paths.
  • Path material choices: Wooden path (wood 1), Stone path (stone 1), Crystal path (crystal 1), Stepping Stone (random). Wooden paths are easiest to obtain, stone paths most durable, crystal paths most luxurious.
  • Path speed bonus: Laying a path boosts movement speed +15-25%, accumulating significant time savings over the long run. Make sure to pave the main paths before winter mining.
  • Curve design: Right-angle turns save space but curves look better. Choose right-angle for efficiency, curves for aesthetics.

🎨 Aesthetics and Decoration Considerations

Even players pursuing efficiency can add aesthetic elements on top of a functional layout, making the farm feel more like "home":

  • Color coordination: Keep path, fence, and building colors unified or complementary. For example, stone paths with stone fences, wooden paths with wood fences.
  • Symmetry and rhythm: Use symmetrical layouts in the planting zone, with buildings arranged symmetrically along the central axis—visually tidier.
  • Seasonal decoration: Swap flower bed colors each season—tulips in spring, sunflowers in summer, pumpkins in fall, holly in winter.
  • Central plaza: Set up a plaza in the farm center with sculptures, fountains, and benches as a visual focal point.
  • Lighting design: Use lanterns and torches at night to accent the main paths—both practical (anti-getting-lost) and beautiful.
  • Water features: Ponds, small bridges, and flowing water significantly enhance the farm's beauty, but avoid taking up too much tillable area.
✅ Balancing Aesthetics and Efficiency

Aesthetic layouts take up 10-20% of tillable area, reducing capacity. We suggest adding decorative elements after the efficiency layout is formed, rather than chasing aesthetics from the start. Decorate on a large scale from year two when funds are ample.

⚙️ Automation-Ready Layout

The core of an automated layout is "letting the machines run themselves." Below are the key considerations when designing an automated layout:

  • Sprinkler matrix: Divide the planting zone neatly according to the iridium sprinkler's 5×5 range, with each sprinkler covering 24 crop tiles. Reserve sprinkler positions to avoid later demolition.
  • Keg clusters: Place kegs in the house cellar (Robin expansion), reserving the surface for planting and processing. The cellar can hold 100+ kegs.
  • Concentrated processing zone: Place mayonnaise machines, cheese presses, Preserves Jars, and oil makers together, adjacent to the shipping bin, forming a "processing line."
  • Auto-feeding: Deluxe coops / barns come with auto-feeders; pair with silos to store hay. Winter hay consumption is high; have at least 2 silos.
  • Mod boosts: After installing the Automate mod, machines can automatically take materials from and output to adjacent chests, truly realizing "idle income." But note this significantly changes game balance.

💡 Evolution Advice
  • Simulate with a farm planner before each major renovation, to avoid destroying the existing layout
  • Preserve core assets (sprinklers, kegs, animals) when renovating—don't demolish and rebuild everything
  • From year two, start planting Ancient Fruit (greenhouse) to prepare for large-scale wine making in year three
  • In year three, consider establishing a second farm on Ginger Island to disperse capacity
  • Layout has no "final form"—it will keep adjusting with your gameplay interests
