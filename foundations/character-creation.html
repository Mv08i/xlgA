🧑‍🌾 Character Creation & Attributes

Before inheriting your grandfather's farm, you need to create your character. Stardew Valley's character creation screen seems full of options, but only a few actually affect the game mechanics—the pet is cosmetic, the favorite thing is an Easter egg, and gender only affects pronouns. The only choice that truly determines opening difficulty is the farm type. This guide breaks down each option's significance, helping you avoid the common pitfall of "overthinking appearance."

👤 Character Appearance Customization In-Depth

When creating a character, you can freely customize appearance. All appearance options have no effect on any attribute values and can be changed for free at any time at the mirror in the Wizard's Tower. This means appearance choices are completely stress-free—if you want a new look later in the game, you can change anytime. Here's an item-by-item breakdown:

  • Player Name: The name NPCs use to address you in-game, appearing in dialogue, letters, and your file. Note that some NPCs have Easter egg reactions to specific names (for example, naming yourself after certain NPCs triggers special lines). It can be changed in the file menu and does not affect your save.
  • Farm Name: Displayed as the farm's identifier on the map, in letters, and on shipping receipts. It cannot be changed later, so name it carefully. The farm name becomes synonymous with your save file—pick something distinctive that you like.
  • Gender: Affects some NPC dialogue wording (like "he/she") and your spouse's form of address, but does not affect abilities or marriage candidates (any gender can pursue any of the 12 candidates). The game is LGBTQ+ friendly, so gender choice is purely personal preference.
  • Skin tone / Hairstyle / Hair color / Shirt / Pants / Accessories: Pure cosmetic customization, changeable anytime at the Wizard's Tower. Accessories include glasses, hats, etc.; more hats can be unlocked through achievements later in the game.
  • Body type preference: From version 1.6 onward, more body type options are supported, also with no effect on any game mechanics.
💡 Appearance Modification

Once you unlock the Wizard's Tower (by completing the Community Center Bundles or buying a Joja membership for 5,000G), the mirror inside lets you adjust all appearance options anytime. So don't agonize over combinations in the early game—just play and decide later. The Wizard's Tower is in the western forest; you must trigger the story event before entering.

✅ Appearance Tips
  • The character creation screen shows a preview animation—try switching between several outfits to see how they look in different actions
  • Hats are the most abundant cosmetic accessory in the late game—completing achievements unlocks dozens of hats
  • Some NPCs have special comment dialogue about your appearance, but it's purely for fun and doesn't affect friendship
  • In multiplayer, each player's appearance is independent and won't conflict

🐱 Pet Choice In-Depth

On Spring 5, Marnie will visit the farm and let you choose one of three pets: Cat, Dog, or Turtle. Once chosen, it cannot be changed—but the good news is that all three pets are completely identical in stats, with no attribute differences. The only differences are appearance, animation, and interaction feedback, purely for emotional companionship. Here's a detailed comparison of each pet's unique behavior to help you pick the one you click with:

🐱 Cat

The classic choice. Daily behavior: loves to lie by the door or on the roof sunbathing, lazily stretching. Purrs when the player approaches; the interaction animation is nuzzling its head against your hand. Hides under the eaves on rainy days and loves to lie by the fireplace in winter. Great for players who enjoy quiet companionship.

🐶 Dog

An energetic companion. Daily behavior: runs around the farm, occasionally chasing butterflies or digging in the dirt. Comes running with wagging tail to welcome you home; the interaction animation is crouching down to pet its head. Some breeds will even bring you sticks to play fetch. Great for players who enjoy a lively atmosphere.

🐢 Turtle

A new option added in version 1.6. Daily behavior: slowly crawls around the farm, often sitting completely still in a daze. When interacted with, it pulls its head into its shell and slowly extends it again—quiet and soothing. The turtle moves slowly, but its activity range matches other pets. Great for players who enjoy a slow pace and a unique choice.

Pet Friendship and Interaction

Once you choose a pet, clicking it once per day lets you pet it, building friendship. When friendship reaches 1000 points (about 20 days of continuous petting), Marnie will visit again and deliver a custom pet bowl, and your pet will get a little home. A pet at max friendship has extra interaction animations—for example, the cat jumps on you, the dog rolls over to show its belly, and the turtle slowly nuzzles your foot. It's worth emphasizing that pet friendship does not affect any income, recipes, or achievements—it's purely emotional investment. If you forget to pet it one day, the pet won't run away from home.

✅ Pet Easter Eggs
  • A pet at max friendship will occasionally bring you small gifts (like wild flowers, small rocks) when you step outside, shown at the door
  • Pets will take shelter from the rain on rainy days, with different expressions when you interact
  • In multiplayer, pets can be interacted with by all players, and friendship is shared

⭐ Favorite Thing In-Depth

When creating a character, there's a "Favorite Thing" input box where you can enter any text or choose from preset options (like Stardew Valley, Coffee, Forest, Universe, Family, Music, etc.). The actual function of this field: when you eat a Stardrop (a rare item that permanently raises your energy cap), the system displays "Your favorite thing is XXX" as a personalized Easter egg line. Beyond that, the "Favorite Thing" does not affect any game mechanics—it doesn't make the corresponding item easier to obtain, nor does it change NPC reactions.

That said, this field is still worth filling in thoughtfully, as it's your character's "personality tag." Many players fill in their real-life hobbies, their partner's name, or a personal motto, and smile every time they eat a Stardrop. From version 1.6 onward, custom text input is supported (no longer limited to preset options), so you can enter anything up to 24 characters.

🎯 Farm Type Is the Real Key to Your Start

Beyond pets, the only opening choice that actually affects game mechanics is "Farm Type." Farm type determines map layout, usable area, special resources, and playstyle—it's the most important decision at the start of the game, and once chosen it cannot be changed (without starting a new file). Many people mistakenly think character appearance or pet affects difficulty, but in reality the farm type's impact far exceeds all other options combined. Here are all the farm types and their positioning:

Farm Type Features Suitable For
🌳 Standard FarmLarge flat area, no special resources, the most traditional farming experienceTop pick for beginners, high tolerance for mistakes
🗺️ Riverland FarmSplit by rivers into multiple small islands, fishing-focusedFishing enthusiasts, fast early money
🌲 Forest FarmOvergrown with trees and weeds; bottom-left has continuously respawning forageables and hardwood stumpsForaging-focused players, strong long-term returns
⚔️ Hill-Top FarmMonsters spawn at night, combat-focusedPlayers who enjoy combat challenges
🌼 Four Corners FarmFour zones each combining the above features, a comprehensive experiencePlayers who want a bit of every playstyle
🏖️ Beach FarmHuge space, forageable sea products, but sprinklers don't work on sandExperienced players seeking a unique challenge
🍄 Meadowlands FarmNew in 1.6; comes with continuously growing grass and movable berry bushesLivestock-focused players, great for sheep and cows
💡 Farm Type Recommendation

For your first playthrough, the "Standard Farm" is highly recommended—large flat area, no special mechanics to interfere, perfect for getting familiar with the game loop. After finishing once, try the Riverland or Forest Farm for a different experience. Farm type has no effect on NPC relationships, festivals, or the main storyline—it only affects your farm's terrain and resources.

No matter how you create your character, starting attributes are fixed—this is Stardew Valley's "equal start" design philosophy:

🚀 Opening Strategy Checklist

Combining the analysis above, here's a clear opening decision checklist for beginners:

🌱 Choose the Standard Farm

For your first playthrough, the Standard Farm is recommended—large flat area, high tolerance, ideal for learning the mechanics. Try other types after finishing the game once.

🐱 Pick a pet you love

All three pets have no attribute differences—just pick the one you like visually. Daily interaction brings emotional value.

⭐ Fill in Favorite Thing freely

Pure Easter egg option—fill in something you love or a personal motto; it doesn't affect any income.

🎒 Buy a backpack first

Save up 2,000G and immediately upgrade your backpack to 24 slots at the General Store—the efficiency boost is immediate and far better value than buying decorations.

🎨 Don't agonize over appearance

Pick anything for now—you can change it for free at the Wizard's Tower later. Your time is better spent farming and fishing.

📌 Choose your farm name carefully

Farm name can't be changed later—pick something distinctive to avoid regret.

💡 About "Perfect Inheritance"

The letter from Grandpa at the start of the game only provides story setup—it doesn't grant any inherited items or special abilities. Your entire estate is 500G, 5 basic tools (Hoe, Axe, Pickaxe, Watering Can, Scythe), and 15 Parsnip seeds. There's no such thing as a "perfect start"—every player begins on the same starting line.

✅ Core Principle of Character Creation

Remember this: 90% of the options on the character creation screen are cosmetic. The only choice that truly affects your game experience is the farm type. Save the time you'd spend agonizing over appearance and put it into researching farm types and early planting plans—the payoff is far greater.
