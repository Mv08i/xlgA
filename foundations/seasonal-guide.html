📅 Four-Season Crops & Festivals

A Stardew Valley year is divided into Spring, Summer, Fall, and Winter—each 28 days, for 112 days total. Crops wither when crossing seasons, and festivals missed mean waiting until next year. Each season has its own crops, forageables, and festival activities. Understanding seasonal patterns is the core of efficient management. This guide compiles recommended crops per season (with detailed analysis), the full festival schedule with prep tips, seasonal strategies, crop rotation planning, and a winter activity guide.

💡 Cross-Season Withering Rule

At dawn of the season-change day, all "current-season crops" wither and cannot be harvested. Greenhouse crops and true cross-season crops (like Ancient Fruit, Cactus) are unaffected. Make sure to harvest everything before the last day of the season. This mechanic means each season is an "independent economic cycle" that requires replanning your planting.

✅ Universal Seasonal Strategies
  • Plant all seeds and water them on day 1 of the season to maximize growing time
  • The earlier you plant multi-harvest crops, the better the returns—try to sow by days 1–3
  • Don't plant crops whose growth cycle exceeds the remaining days of the season—it wastes seeds
  • Shops are closed on festival days—stock up on seeds and gifts the day before

🌸 Spring

Spring is the opening season of each year and a critical time for beginners to learn the mechanics. Funds are tight, tools are basic, and the fields are barren—but it's also the season with the greatest potential. Choosing the right crops lets you accumulate enough startup capital for Summer within 28 days.

✅ Spring Strategy In-Depth

The Egg Festival is the turning point of Spring. For the first 13 days, focus on Parsnips and Potatoes to save money; on the festival day, make sure to find all 9 eggs to trade for Strawberry seeds. Plant the Strawberries immediately after the festival (15 days left: 8 days to mature + 4 days to regrow = at least 2 harvests, at 120G each). If you have spare capacity, plant another round of Cauliflower (plant by Spring 16 to harvest before day 28). Spring is also a great time to forage Leeks, Dandelions, and Daffodils to supplement early income.

☀️ Summer

Summer is the starting point of the brewing industry chain. Funds start to free up, allowing large-scale planting of high-value brewing ingredients. Summer crops overall yield higher profit than Spring's, but require more upfront investment (expensive seeds, need kegs).

✅ Summer Strategy In-Depth

The Blueberry + Hops combo is the top pick for Summer. Blueberries suit large-field planting (4 harvests × 3 berries = 12 berries per plant—astonishing profit when brewed); Hops have high density but need trellises, suitable for small-scale intensive farming. If you don't have enough kegs, prioritize turning Blueberries into wine and Hops into Pale Ale. Save a few Melons for gold-quality quests and Fair stock. Sunflowers paired with bee houses are a great passive income choice.

✅ Fall Strategy & Fair Tips

The Pumpkin + Cranberry combo is the top pick for Fall. Brew Pumpkins into wine and continuously harvest Cranberries. The Fair is the most important festival of the year—prepare exhibits a week in advance: fill 9 slots with diverse gold/iridium quality items (e.g., gold-quality Pumpkin, iridium-quality Mayonnaise, gold-quality Catfish, iridium-quality Diamond), score over 1000 for first prize, and exchange star tokens for a Stardrop to raise your energy cap.

❄️ Winter

Winter has no outdoor crops—it's a season for rest, socializing, and pushing through the mines. Many beginners find Winter boring, but it's actually a golden period for leveling skills, accumulating rare resources, and reorganizing your farm. No watering means all your energy can go to other activities.

Complete Winter Activity Guide

  • ⛏️ Mining (highest priority): No watering needed, so put all your energy into the mines. Aim to reach level 120 for Iridium Ore, Diamonds, and a Stardrop (chest on level 100). Bring combat dishes like Spicy Eel and Crab Cakes, plus staircases and bombs.
  • 🎣 Fishing: Winter has unique fish species (like Halibut, Sardine), and you can fish all day without watering. The Desert Trader accepts Frozen Geodes in exchange for rare items. The Festival of Ice (Winter 8) also has a fishing contest.
  • 🌲 Foraging: Winter forageables include Crystal Fruit (10 per Winter, can be made into Geodes), Holly, and Crocus. The forest and farm surroundings continuously refresh, supplementing income and helping complete the Foraging Bundle.
  • 👥 Socializing: Concentrate gift-giving to boost NPC friendship. Villagers are mostly indoors in Winter, making them easy to find. Memorize the birthday list—missing a birthday is a big loss.
  • 🔨 Tool upgrades: Take advantage of the off-season to upgrade your Watering Can and other tools without disrupting planting. Prioritize the Watering Can and Pickaxe—iridium tools are a qualitative leap.
  • 🏗️ Farm reorganization: Clear weeds, plan fields, lay paths and fences, place sprinklers. Prepare for next Spring.
  • 🏗️ Construction upgrades: Hire Robin to build barns, silos, greenhouses, and other buildings—Winter is construction season.

🔄 Rotation Planning & Cross-Season Strategy

Stardew Valley's "rotation" isn't crop rotation in the agricultural sense, but rather planting planning at season transitions. The core principle: keep each season's fields busy while preparing for the next season.

  • Spring → Summer transition: Harvest the last batch of Strawberries before Summer 1; plant Blueberries and Hops on Summer day 1
  • Summer → Fall transition: Last Blueberry harvest before Fall 1; plant Pumpkins and Cranberries on Fall day 1
  • Fall → Winter transition: Harvest all Fall crops; pivot to mining and socializing in Winter
  • Winter → Spring transition: Get fields ready in Winter; plant Parsnips or Cauliflower on Spring day 1

Cross-Season Crop Exceptions: Ancient Fruit (planted in Spring, harvested in Summer and Fall, every 7 days), Cactus (Desert exclusive) are not season-limited, and greenhouse crops never wither. So prioritize planting high-value cross-season crops like Ancient Fruit or Starfruit in the greenhouse.

💡 Foraging Tips

When the Foraging skill reaches level 5, choose "Forester" to make foraged goods automatically iridium quality; at level 10, "Botanist" makes all foraged goods iridium quality. The Secret Woods (requires a Steel Axe to chop the stumps) refreshes large amounts of Hardwood and Mushrooms daily—a high-value foraging spot.

💡 General Festival Tips
  • Shops are closed on festival days—stock up on seeds, gifts, and needed items the day before
  • Entering a festival scene auto-jumps time to the end of the festival (usually 10:00 PM), so no need to worry about wasted time
  • Missing a festival means waiting until next year—it won't affect main story progress
  • Do farm chores before leaving for a festival, since time will jump forward
  • The Fair and Night Market are the year's most worthwhile festivals—plan ahead
