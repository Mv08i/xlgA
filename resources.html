📚 Resources

Here's a curated list of the most useful external resources, tools, and communities for Stardew Valley players. Stardew Valley is a deeply rich game, and this site only covers core gameplay and strategy decisions—a lot of in-depth data, visualization tools, and player communities live in external resources. Below, they're organized by use case, with notes on when each is the right fit and what its pros and cons are.

🌐 Official Resources

Official resources are the most authoritative source of information. We recommend checking them first for any specific data (like item sale prices, growth cycles, or version updates). Note that official resources are primarily in English; community translations may lag behind.

The game's official website, for the latest news and to purchase the game. The site posts version update announcements, new content previews, and ConcernedApe's dev logs. If you want to know "what's coming in the next version," this is your first-hand source.

🛠️ Practical Tools

These tools can dramatically boost your game efficiency, covering everything from crop selection to farm planning. Treat them as "decision aids," not "answer generators"—the tools give you data, but the final call still has to factor in your playstyle and current stage.

💰 Profit Calculator

Calculates the planting profit of different crops to help you pick the most lucrative ones. The core logic of a profit calculator combines seed cost, growth cycle, number of harvests per season, quality probability, processing bonuses, and other factors to give a "daily profit per crop."

How to use: Enter the current season, target planting area, sprinkler setup, and skill profession (Rancher / Tiller), and the calculator will output a crop comparison sorted by "daily gold per tile." Note the difference between "shipping profit" and "processing profit"—the same crop's wine might triple the profit, but it needs an extra 7 days of processing time.

Use the calculator →
🗺️ Farm Planner

A visual tool for planning your farm layout and trying out different designs. The farm planner is a web tool that supports drag-and-drop placement of sprinklers, planting areas, buildings, fences, and paths, and shows coverage and tillable area in real time. Simulate in the planner before you rebuild your farm to avoid wrecking an existing layout.

Recommended scenarios: When switching from a compact layout to an automated one, when planning a large Keg matrix, or when designing an aesthetic estate. Screenshots exported from the planner can also be shared with the community for feedback.

View plans →
📅 Seasonal Calendar

An overview of each season's crop growth cycles, festivals, and events. The seasonal calendar is the most important time-management tool for beginners—it tells you which day to plant, which day a festival falls on, and which day crops will wither.

Key dates: You must finish planting by day 1 of each season (missing a day means one fewer harvest), the Egg Festival on day 13, the Luau on summer day 11, the Stardew Valley Fair on fall day 16, and the Night Market on winter day 15.

View calendar →
🎣 Fishing Guide

Spawn conditions, locations, and best fishing times for every fish. Fishing is one of Stardew Valley's most complex subsystems—the same fish has different spawn chances across seasons, weather, time of day, and locations.

Usage tips: Check the guide before fishing to confirm a target fish's spawn conditions, so you don't waste time catching the wrong thing. Rare fish needed for the Community Center "Fish Bundles" (like the Legend and the Scorpion Carp) must be looked up in advance.

🎬 Video Guide Channels

Video guides are great for learning specific operation rhythms and decision processes—they're more intuitive than text guides. Here are a few widely recognized high-quality English channels (high-quality video guides in other languages are relatively scarce; feel free to recommend more in the community):

  • DangerouslyFunny / Silver: Focused on comedic gameplay experiments and challenges, great for entertainment viewing and inspiration for non-mainstream playstyles.
  • Wiki companion videos: Some wiki entries come with short demo videos, good for seeing specific operations (like fishing rhythm or mine routes).
  • Speedrun community: Searching YouTube for "Stardew Valley speedrun" surfaces plenty of speedrun videos where you can learn the operation logic under extreme efficiency—though everyday play doesn't need to copy them.
💡 Tips for Using Video Guides

Videos are good for "watching the rhythm," not for "copying the homework." A streamer's hit run depends on their reaction speed and luck, and ordinary players shouldn't force a replication. When watching, focus on the order of their decisions and how they allocate resources, not the specific numbers.

🔌 Mod Recommendations (Use With Care)

Mods can hugely expand Stardew Valley's gameplay, but they also shift the balance. We recommend finishing at least one mod-free playthrough before trying mods, so you don't lose the vanilla experience. Below are widely recognized high-quality mods with strong vanilla compatibility:

  • SMAPI: The prerequisite loader for every mod—must be installed first.
  • CJB Cheats Menu: For debugging—can modify time, money, and skill levels. Good for testing layouts or screenshots; not recommended for daily use.
  • Lookup Anything: Press F1 to look up detailed info on any item / NPC (friendship, birthday, gift preferences). The most useful helper mod, with almost no balance impact.
  • UI Info Suite: Shows the day's birthdays, traveling cart inventory, crop status, and more on the HUD—drastically cuts menu-surfing time.
  • Automate: Lets machines automatically pull materials from and output to adjacent chests—the core mod for automated layouts.
  • Stardew Valley Expanded: A large content expansion mod that adds NPCs, maps, and storylines. Great for finding a fresh experience after beating the base game.
⚠️ Mod Notes
  • Always back up your save before installing mods—mod conflicts can corrupt saves.
  • Mods may break after every version update; you'll need to wait for the mod author to adapt.
  • In multiplayer, all players must install the same mods, or you can't enter the game.
  • Achievements usually can't be unlocked on modded saves.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What's the difference between this site and the official wiki?

The wiki is a "dictionary"; this site is a "tutorial." The wiki tells you Blueberry's sale price and growth cycle; this site tells you "with 5,000G in summer of Year One, should you plant all blueberries or diversify." They work best together.

Q: Should I install mods?

We recommend finishing at least one mod-free playthrough. The vanilla content is rich enough that installing mods too early will cost you the joy of discovery. Helper mods like Lookup Anything and UI Info Suite are fine to use, but content expansion mods (like SVE) are best saved for after you've beaten the base game.

Q: Is there a big gap between the profit calculator and actual earnings?

The calculator gives the theoretical optimum. Actual earnings will deviate by about 10–20% due to luck, weather, and how practiced you are. Use the calculator to compare crops side by side, not to predict exact gold.

Q: Can a multiplayer save be converted back to single-player?

A multiplayer save can be continued solo by the host player, but other players' characters will sit in their cabins unplayable. We recommend confirming every player's time commitment before starting multiplayer, to avoid mid-game dropouts hurting the experience.
