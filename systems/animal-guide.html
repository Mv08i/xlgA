🐔 Complete Animal Husbandry Guide

Animal husbandry is a stable passive income source in the mid and late game. This guide covers coop and barn animals, product processing, building costs, and daily care essentials to help you build an efficient animal farm.

🏗️ Buildings and Costs

All animal husbandry buildings are constructed by the carpenter Robin and require gold and materials. Once built, you can purchase animals from Marnie.

✅ King of Truffles

💖 Animal Care and Daily Routine

The core of animal husbandry is not "buy an animal and get output," but "raise the animal's mood and friendship to maximum." Mood determines whether that day's output is a large product, and friendship determines the product's quality distribution (regular / silver / gold / iridium). A fully befriended, full-mood animal can produce 2-3 times the daily value of a beginner's animal. Daily care must form a stable morning-and-evening rhythm.

🌾 Daily Feeding (Morning)

Each animal needs 1 portion of Hay per day. In spring and summer, you can open the door to let them eat fresh grass outside (free); in autumn, winter, or on rainy days, you must dispense Hay from the Silo (50G per portion, requires cutting grass to stock up). Missing one day of feeding zeroes out mood, requiring 3 days to recover.

🤝 Daily Petting (Morning)

Click on each animal daily to pet it and raise friendship—friendship ranges from 0-1000 points, corresponding to 5 hearts. At max friendship, product quality stably stays at gold star or above. Build the habit of "petting each one as you enter."

🚪 Open Door on Sunny Days (6 AM)

Letting animals outside to graze saves Hay and boosts mood and friendship. Open the door right after 6 AM and close it before 5 PM to avoid nighttime wildlife scares that lower mood.

🔥 Winter Heating (All Day)

Install a Heater in the building (2000G each from Robin) to maintain temperature in winter and prevent mood drops. One Heater is enough to cover a building; it's essential for winter production.

❤️ Friendship and Mood System Deep Dive

Animals have two independent values: Friendship (long-term accumulation) and Mood (daily fluctuation). Understanding the difference between the two is key to high-end animal husbandry. Friendship determines product quality (silver / gold / iridium star chance), while Mood determines product size (regular / large). Both must be maxed to reliably produce the highest-value iridium-star large products.

✅ Full-Status Animal Returns

A chicken at max friendship + max mood can have a 50%+ chance of producing a Large Iridium Egg, with the per-unit sale price jumping from 50G to 190G (iridium ×2); a full-status Pig reliably produces 1-3 Truffles per day. Build the morning-and-evening habit and you can raise animals to full status in 30 days.

⭐ Product Quality Factors

Animal products also come in four quality tiers—regular, silver, gold, and iridium—with the same sale price multipliers as crops (×1 / 1.25 / 1.5 / 2.0). Quality is directly determined by Friendship: 0-200 is almost all regular, 200-500 starts showing silver, 500-800 shows gold, and only 800+ yields iridium. Processed products (Mayonnaise, Cheese) are fixed at regular quality, but their sale price already includes the processing value-add, so raw materials from high-friendship animals are more profitable after processing (because gold/iridium raw materials used for wine/oil get extra bonuses).

  • Petting is the top priority: Pet every day—even just once is better than not at all.
  • Going outside to graze gives double benefit: It raises both friendship and mood simultaneously, the most cost-effective care action.
  • Winter Heater is essential: Without a Heater, animal mood zeroes out, and even at max friendship they only produce regular quality.

🏗️ Building Upgrades and Unlock Path

Both Coop and Barn have three tiers: Basic, Big, and Deluxe. Upgrades must be done sequentially (no skipping), and each upgrade doubles capacity, unlocks new animal types, and adds an Auto-Feeder function. When planning upgrade paths, simultaneously consider the animal purchase budget and processing equipment matching to avoid the awkward situations of "pens but no animals" or "animals but no processing."

💡 Auto-Feeder

Big Coop and above, and Big Barn and above, come with an Auto-Feeder system: as long as there's Hay in the Silo, it will be dispensed automatically each morning. This means rainy days and winter no longer require manual feeding, greatly reducing daily workload. However, on sunny days you should still open the door to let animals graze, saving Hay reserves.

🐑 Sheep Shearing Cycle

Sheep produce Wool, but you must shear them manually with Shears (unlike chickens and cows, which produce automatically). At max friendship, Wool grows back about every 3 days; a regular bundle sells for 340G, or 375G when processed into Cloth. Combined with the "Shepherd" profession branch (the opposite branch of Coopmaster, chosen at Farming level 10), Wool grows faster—a stable mid-tier income source in the late game.

  • Shearing timing: When the Wool is fully grown, a distinct white fleece appears on the sheep—click to shear.
  • Cloth processing: The Loom takes 4 hours to finish; it's recommended to have 1 Loom per sheep to avoid raw-material buildup.
  • Role: Sheep profit is mid-tier—mainly for social gifts (Emily loves Cloth) and textile material. Not recommended as a primary income animal.

🐷 Pig Truffle Hunting Guide

Pigs are the highest daily-profit animal per head in Stardew Valley, but a few often-overlooked details directly affect returns. Pigs only find Truffles when they go outside on sunny days—they completely stop production on rainy days and in winter. Truffles appear randomly on outdoor tiles around the pig and must be picked up manually. At max friendship, a pig produces 1-3 Truffles per day, averaging 1.5.

✅ Pig Pen Operations Tips
  • Clear a large open area around the Barn (no obstacles) to give pigs more space to find Truffles.
  • Allocate 1 Oil Maker per 2 pigs to avoid Truffle buildup and selling raw (losing 440G per Truffle).
  • Choose the "Shepherd" profession to boost Truffle quality, greatly increasing iridium-star chance.
  • Consider selling some pigs before winter for cash, but max-friendship pigs are not recommended to sell (high rebuilding cost).

🤖 Automated Operations: From Dawn to Dusk to a 5-Minute Routine

The ultimate goal of animal husbandry is "passive income"—spending only 5 minutes a day to complete all care. Reaching this goal requires three layers of automation: the first layer is Auto-Feeding (included in Big Coop / Big Barn and above), where Hay is automatically dispensed from the Silo with no manual work; the second layer is scaled-up processing equipment, with 1 corresponding machine per animal so raw materials enter the pipeline the same day; the third layer is building placement optimization, putting the Coop / Barn near the farm entrance with processing equipment clustered along the path, for a minimalist "pet on entry, collect on exit" flow.

After completing all three layers, the daily routine for 12 pigs is just: open the door at 6 AM (5 seconds) → pet 12 pigs (30 seconds) → collect Truffles (1 minute) → load Oil Makers (30 seconds), totaling under 3 minutes—yet daily profit can exceed 7500G. That is the final form of Stardew Valley animal husbandry.

🎯 Beginner Animal Husbandry Roadmap

  1. Late spring, year 1: Save 4,000G + 300 Wood + 100 Stone, build a Basic Coop
  2. Summer: Buy 4 Chickens (prioritize Brown), build 4 Mayonnaise Machines, pay back in 10 days
  3. Fall: Upgrade to a Big Barn, introduce Cows and Goats, add Cheese Presses
  4. Winter: Complete the Deluxe Barn, start introducing Pigs (buy 2-4 to test the waters first)
  5. Spring, year 2: 12 Pigs at full capacity, with 6 Oil Makers, entering the passive-income golden age
  6. Summer, year 2: Expand to a second Deluxe Barn, double the Pigs, daily average breaking 15,000G
