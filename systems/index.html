🌱 Planting System

Planting is one of Stardew Valley's core gameplay loops and the main source of income in the early and mid game. A mature planting system must simultaneously account for soil, fertilizer, crop variety, growth cycle, and processing chain, rather than simply tossing seeds into the ground. Understanding the output efficiency of each tile is the only way to truly convert your energy and time into gold.

🧪 Soil and Fertilizer System

Soil itself does not lose fertility, but fertilizer determines the quality distribution and growth speed of crops. Fertilizer is applied before sowing and persists until the next season's crops rot, making it the hidden core of high-end planting. The four common fertilizers each have their own role: Basic Fertilizer (recipe: 2 Sap) only increases the chance of gold-star drops for regular crops, is cheap, and is suited for large-scale use in the first year; Quality Fertilizer (recipe: 2 Sap + 1 Fish, unlocked at Farming level 9) significantly increases the iridium-star chance and is the main choice in the late game; Speed-Gro (recipe: 1 Truffle Oil + 1 Sap) boosts crop growth speed by 10%, while Deluxe Speed-Gro boosts it by 25%, clearly amplifying returns on single-harvest high-value crops (such as Starfruit and Pumpkin); Basic Retaining Soil keeps the soil moist overnight, saving enormous energy in the early game when you have no sprinklers, but it is replaced by Quality Sprinklers in the mid and late game.

💡 Fertilizer Pairing Principles

For multi-harvest crops (Blueberry, Cranberry, Grape), prioritize Quality Fertilizer, since the quality bonus compounds across every harvest; for single-harvest high-value crops (Starfruit, Sweet Gem Berry), prioritize Deluxe Speed-Gro—squeezing in one extra harvest cycle is one extra round of huge profit.

⭐ Crop Quality Grading

Crop quality directly determines sale price: regular (×1), silver star (×1.25), gold star (×1.5), iridium star (×2.0). Quality is jointly determined by fertilizer and Farming skill level. At Farming level 10 with Quality Fertilizer, the iridium-star chance can exceed 50%, and the overall average price is about 60% higher than regular crops. This is why a high-skill player's output from the same plot can far exceed a beginner's—skill and fertilizer are invisible profit multipliers.

📊 Crop Selection Framework: Daily Profit Method

When choosing crops, you cannot look only at per-plant sale price—you must calculate "daily profit." The formula is: (total seasonal sale price - total seed cost) ÷ total days in the season. For example, a Strawberry seed costs 100G, matures in 8 days, then produces a harvest every 4 days (120G). Over a 28-day season you can harvest about 6 times, giving a per-plant total sale price of 720G. Subtracting the 100G seed cost, the daily profit is about 22G/day, far higher than ordinary crops. Beyond daily profit, you also need to layer in risk assessment and continuous-harvest strategy.

  • Risk Analysis: Single-harvest crops (Cauliflower, Pumpkin) suffer heavy losses from crows or a missed watering, so they need scarecrows; multi-harvest crops (Blueberry, Cranberry) only lose a small fraction even if one harvest is missed, so their fault tolerance is high.
  • Continuous-Harvest Strategy: Multi-harvest crops like Blueberry, Hot Pepper, Cranberry, and Grape, once planted early, can produce for the entire season, saving the energy and time of repeatedly buying and sowing seeds. They are the best choice for mid-game expansion.
  • Seed Availability: Strawberry seeds can only be bought at the Spring 13 Egg Festival; Starfruit and Sweet Gem Berry seeds require unlocking the Desert. These time points must be factored into first-year planning.

🐔 Animal Husbandry Economics

Animal husbandry is an important passive income source in the mid and late game. Compared with planting, animal husbandry requires higher upfront investment in buildings and animals, but daily care takes only a few minutes morning and night, and once output stabilizes, the payback period is about one season. What really makes money is not selling raw materials, but processing products into mayonnaise, cheese, cloth, and truffle oil—after processing, profit typically doubles or even triples.

📋 Animal Types and Output

🐔 White Chicken / Brown Chicken

Purchase price 800G. Produces Egg (50G each); Brown Chicken produces Large Egg (95G). Processed into mayonnaise at 190G / 275G respectively. Daily output, fastest payback.

🐄 Cow

Purchase price 1500G. Produces Milk (125G/bottle); cheese press turns it into Cheese (230G). Daily output, the top choice for a starter barn.

🐑 Sheep

Purchase price 2000G. Produces Wool (340G/bundle); loom turns it into Cloth (375G). Produces once every 3 days, requires a Big Barn.

🦆 Duck

Purchase price 1200G. Produces Duck Egg (95G) or Duck Feather (250G), once every 2 days. Duck Feathers are a universally liked NPC gift.

🐰 Rabbit

Purchase price 8000G. Produces Wool (340G) or Rabbit's Foot (1000G), about once every 4 days. Rabbit's Foot is a top-tier gift.

🐷 Pig (Late-Game King)

Purchase price 16000G. Finds Truffles outdoors on sunny days (625G each); oil maker turns them into Truffle Oil (1065G). A single pig's daily profit can exceed 1000G.

🔄 Product Processing Chain

The key to getting rich from animals is not selling raw materials but processing them. Egg → Mayonnaise Machine → Mayonnaise (price jumps from 50G to 190G); Milk → Cheese Press → Cheese (from 125G to 230G); Truffle → Oil Maker → Truffle Oil (from 625G to 1065G). It is recommended to configure at least 4-6 corresponding processing machines per animal type, so raw materials can enter the processing pipeline the same day. The Artisan skill (+40% processed-good sale price) takes the returns of this chain up another notch.

📊 Animal Husbandry ROI Calculation

Take "4 Chickens + 4 Mayonnaise Machines" as an example: investment of 3200G (chickens) + 4000G (Basic Coop) + a small amount of materials comes to just over 7000G. Daily output of 4 Eggs → 4 Mayonnaise = 760G/day, paying back in about 10 days. By contrast, "12 Pigs + Deluxe Barn" requires about 220,000G total investment, but daily profit can exceed 7500G, paying back within a single season and becoming pure passive income thereafter. Beginners should prioritize a coop for a quick cash recovery, then gradually expand to a barn with pigs.

📅 Time Management Philosophy

A Stardew Valley day runs from 6 AM to a forced sleep at 2 AM, giving roughly 20 hours of usable time. How you allocate these 20 hours determines the speed of your farm's development. The core idea is "layered processing by priority," pouring limited time and energy into the activities with the highest output.

  1. Must-do items (morning 6:00-10:00): Watering, harvesting, petting and feeding animals. These are non-skippable routines, because a missed watering delays crop growth and a missed feeding lowers animal mood.
  2. Productive activities (10:00 AM-4:00 PM): Mining, fishing, foraging. This is the main battlefield for energy and time. Pick one main direction per season—fishing in spring, mining in summer and autumn, deep mining in winter.
  3. Social time (4:00 PM-6:00 PM): Deliver gifts and bulletin-board quests before NPCs leave work. Prioritize "loved" gifts—twice a week steadily advances friendship.
  4. Project planning (6:00 PM-10:00 PM): Farm expansion, inventory organizing, product processing. The mines are closed and NPCs are home in the evening, making it a window for organizing and decision-making.
  5. Wrap-up and rest (after 10:00 PM): Avoid staying outdoors after midnight, or your energy the next day will drop sharply. Healthy management matters more than overtime.

🍂 Seasonal Energy Allocation

In spring, prioritize energy for watering (no sprinklers early on) and spend the remaining time fishing to recover cash; in summer, as crop area expands, watering takes a larger share, so craft Quality Sprinklers as soon as possible to free up energy; in autumn, the profit peak, energy should focus on harvesting and processing, with mining as a secondary activity; in winter, with no watering needed, it is the golden period to push the mines to floor 120, unlock the Desert, and farm iridium ore. Grasping this kind of seasonal energy reallocation is more efficient than simply piling on hours.

💰 Gold Flow: Investment vs. Savings

The most common beginner mistake is "hoarding money instead of investing." Gold in Stardew Valley only appreciates when converted into productive capacity (land, sprinklers, processing equipment, animals); idle gold is depreciating gold. To judge when to invest and when to keep cash on hand, follow three principles:

  • Keep 20% as emergency fund: For tool upgrades, unexpected events, and festival spending (such as buying Strawberry seeds at the Egg Festival).
  • Prioritize fast-payback investments: Basic Coop (7000G, 10-day payback) > Barn expansion > Tool upgrades > Large buildings.
  • Milestone investments: At 20,000G, buy a second barn to expand; at 50,000G, build a pig pen; at 100,000G, invest in Keg industrialization.

Conversely, avoid "over-investing in buildings." For example, a Deluxe Barn that only houses 4 cows pays back much more slowly than a Basic Barn. Every building should be paired with a fully stocked animal population and corresponding processing equipment to count as a complete investment.

🤖 Advanced Automation Path

Moving from pure manual labor to high automation is the core growth line of Stardew Valley's mid and late game. The recommended progression is as follows: Phase 1 (spring-summer) use Basic Sprinklers to cover 4 tiles, freeing up partial energy; Phase 2 (summer-autumn) unlock Quality Sprinklers (Farming level 6), each covering 8 tiles, so a 30-50 tile farm needs almost no hand watering; Phase 3 (autumn-winter) build large numbers of Kegs and Preserves Jars to automatically convert crops into high-value products; Phase 4 (year 2) introduce Iridium Sprinklers (covering 24 tiles) and auto-feeding buildings, putting the farm into a "5-minute morning routine + concentrated harvesting" passive mode; Phase 5 (long-term) plant the Greenhouse entirely with Ancient Fruit + a Keg matrix, achieving pure passive income of thousands of G per day. Each step is built on the stable payback of the previous one—do not skip ahead.
