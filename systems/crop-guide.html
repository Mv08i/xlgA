🌱 Detailed Crop Guide

This guide collects the growth cycles, seed prices, sale prices, and daily profits of all major crops across the four seasons to help you plan each season's planting scientifically. Profit data is calculated based on regular quality with no bonuses.

💡 Profit Notes

Daily profit = (sale price × number of harvests - seed price) ÷ total growth days. Multi-harvest crops are calculated over a full 28-day season, including the initial growth + multiple regrowths. High-quality and processed products (wine, pickles) can be significantly higher than the values in the table.

⭐ Crop Quality System

In Stardew Valley, every harvested crop carries a quality grade that directly determines the sale price multiplier. Regular quality is the base price, silver star ×1.25, gold star ×1.5, iridium star ×2.0. Iridium-star crops are also often used as NPC "loved" gifts or premium options for Community Center bundles. Quality is jointly determined by two factors: Farming skill level and the fertilizer used.

✅ Quality Optimization Tips
  • Multi-harvest crops (Blueberry, Cranberry, Grape) must use Quality Fertilizer—the quality bonus compounds across every harvest.
  • Single-harvest crops (Pumpkin, Starfruit) paired with the Artisan skill for wine can use regular quality, since the processed product has a fixed price.
  • Save iridium-star crops for gifts or display; process regular crops first, applying the quality advantage where it matters.

🧪 Complete Fertilizer Guide

Fertilizer is applied to the soil before sowing and determines the crop's quality distribution or growth speed. The same fertilizer persists until the next season's crops rot, so you can lay it down at the end of the previous season. There are four types of fertilizer, each with clear use cases:

💡 Fertilizer and Sprinkler Conflicts

Fertilizer can coexist with sprinklers: apply the fertilizer first, then place the sprinkler, then sow. Note that once fertilizer is applied, removing the soil will remove the fertilizer too, so plan the layout before operating.

💡 Ancient Fruit

Ancient Seeds are obtained by donating to the Museum. After planting, they mature in 28 days and then produce fruit every 7 days. Brewed into wine, the unit price is 2,310G—the king of late-game passive income. Planting the entire Greenhouse with Ancient Fruit is recommended.

🗺️ Crop Planting Layout Strategy

Planting layout is not just about putting crops in the ground—it must also account for watering efficiency, harvest paths, processing equipment placement, and scarecrow coverage. A good layout can save you more than 30% of your daily energy and time. Three core principles:

  1. Sprinkler-first layout: Plan 3×3 (Basic Sprinkler), 5×5 (Quality Sprinkler), or 7×7 (Iridium Sprinkler) planting zones centered on the sprinkler. Place the sprinkler in the center with crops in a ring around it, maximizing coverage per sprinkler.
  2. Scarecrow coverage planning: A regular scarecrow covers a 17×17 area (about 288 tiles); a Deluxe Scarecrow covers even more. Concentrate planting zones within scarecrow coverage and fill the edges with fruit trees or decorations to prevent crows from stealing crops.
  3. Paths and processing equipment nearby: Leave a 1-tile-wide path between the planting zone and Kegs, Preserves Jars, and storage for quick harvesting and loading of raw materials. Processing equipment should be clustered in the center of the farm or next to the Greenhouse to minimize running around.
✅ Classic 3×3 Layout Template

With a Quality Sprinkler at the center, plant crops in the 8 surrounding tiles; four 3×3 blocks share one scarecrow. This layout requires only 4 sprinklers + 1 scarecrow per 36-tile planting zone, the most common mid-scale solution for summer and fall of year one.

🏠 Greenhouse Planting Guide

The Greenhouse is the only place in Stardew Valley where you can plant year-round. Once restored, it has a 12×10 planting area of 120 tiles and requires no watering (built-in irrigation). The Greenhouse is the core of late-game passive income—used correctly, daily income can exceed 5000G. The two core choices for Greenhouse planting are Ancient Fruit vs. Starfruit.

Crop Growth Cycle Per-Plant Sale Price Wine Sale Price Daily Profit (Full Greenhouse)

Hops11 days / 3-day regrow25G300G (Pale Ale)~3,600G/day
💡 Greenhouse Choice Tips
  • Ancient Fruit: Seeds are rare (require Museum donation); after the initial 28-day maturation, it's zero-maintenance, suited to players seeking stable passive income. Patiently cultivate 30-50 plants early as a seed bank.
  • Starfruit: Seeds can be bought at the Desert shop (400G each); highest profit but requires continuous replanting, suited to players pursuing maximum income. Pair with Deluxe Speed-Gro to further compress the growth cycle.
  • Hops: Solid daily profit but requires daily harvesting + trellis, with high energy consumption; not recommended for large-scale planting.
  • The Greenhouse doesn't need sprinklers or scarecrows, so the saved resources can be redirected to outdoor farm expansion.

🍷 Processing Deep Dive: Which Crops Are Worth Processing?

Processing is the core link in crop value-add, but not all crops are worth processing. The judgment criterion is "profit per unit time": a Keg has a 7-day cycle, a Preserves Jar a 3-day cycle. With the same +125% value-add, the Preserves Jar's turnover is 2.3 times that of the Keg, so low-value crops are actually more cost-effective in Preserves Jars, while high-value crops yield higher absolute value in Kegs.

✅ Processing Equipment Ratio

A 2:1 ratio of Kegs to Preserves Jars is recommended. Kegs handle high-value crops (Starfruit, Ancient Fruit, Pumpkin); Preserves Jars handle low-value crops (Blueberry, Cranberry, Potato). Combined with the Artisan skill (+40% processed-good sale price), the overall processing return climbs another notch. With a full Greenhouse of Ancient Fruit, you need at least 60 Kegs to digest the output.
