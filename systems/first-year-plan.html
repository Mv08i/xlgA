📅 Complete First-Year Plan

A detailed roadmap from spring to winter to help you smoothly navigate your first year and lay a solid foundation for year two. This plan breaks tasks down by week, highlights key milestones and common pitfalls, and is suitable for first-time Stardew Valley players to follow step by step.

💡 Planning Philosophy

The core goal of the first year is not to make the most money, but to advance four lines "in parallel": cash flow, Farming skill, Community Center restoration, and relationships. Neglecting any one of them will cause a bottleneck in year two. Review your progress at the end of each week and use the table below to spot any gaps.

🌸 Spring — Starting Out and Laying Foundations

Spring is the starter season for new players. The focus is on learning basic mechanics, accumulating starting funds, and unlocking the Mines and Community Center. The 15 Parsnip seeds at the start of spring are free startup capital, and never miss Willy's fishing rod quest.

Spring Key Goals

  • Learn basic operations: planting, watering, harvesting, fishing
  • Accumulate 2000-5000G in starting funds
  • Unlock the Mines after Spring 5; stock up on Strawberry seeds at the Spring 13 Egg Festival
  • Complete the Community Center Spring Foraging Bundle (requires 4 spring forageables)
  • Upgrade the Watering Can to Copper (5-day build time; submit on a rainy day or Friday)
  • Build initial friendship (2 hearts each) with at least 5 NPCs

Common Beginner Pitfalls in Spring

  • No scarecrow: Without a scarecrow, crows will eat a large number of crops. Have Robin build one right at the start.
  • Running out of energy: Beginners often water down to 0 energy and pass out, wasting 1000G on medical fees. Learn to keep 20 energy in reserve to get home.
  • Timing tool upgrades: You cannot water for 5 days while the Watering Can is being upgraded. Choose a stretch of rainy days or submit on Friday (the weekend plus Monday covers it nicely).
  • Forgetting festivals: Spring 13 Egg Festival and Spring 24 Flower Dance are must-attend—missing them is a big loss.

☀️ Summer — Expansion and Stabilization

Summer is the harvest season. The focus is on expanding production, obtaining sprinklers, and introducing animal husbandry. Summer crop profits are significantly higher than spring, making it the key stage for accumulating capital in year one.

Summer Key Goals

  • Expand farmland to 50-80 tiles; introduce Quality Sprinklers (Farming level 6)
  • Build the first Coop (4000G); introduce 4 Chickens
  • Total gold reaches 30000G+
  • Push the Mines to floor 80 and unlock Gold Ore
  • Upgrade tools to Iron (prioritize the Hoe and Axe)
  • Complete the Community Center Summer Crop Bundle and Construction Bundle

Common Summer Pitfalls

  • Forgetting to save Blueberry seeds: Blueberry seeds can only be bought on Summer 1. In year one, poor planning often delays planting until Summer 3, losing two harvests.
  • Building sprinklers too late: Quality Sprinklers aren't unlocked until Farming level 6. Use Basic Sprinklers as a stopgap early on—don't wait until autumn to make them.
  • Forgetting to feed hay in the Coop: In summer, animals eat grass outside and are fine, but on rainy days when they can't go out, you must put hay inside, or their mood will crash.

🍂 Fall — Profit Maximization

Fall is the most profitable season of year one. The focus is on maximizing income, expanding animal husbandry, and advancing the Community Center. The Pumpkin and Cranberry combo can push a single season's income past 50,000G.

Fall Key Goals

  • Total gold reaches 80000G+
  • Build a Barn; introduce Cows and Goats
  • Upgrade tools to Gold (prioritize the Hoe)
  • Complete most of the Community Center (including the Fall Crop Bundle and Quality Fish Bundle)
  • Plant the Pumpkin + Cranberry golden combo
  • Unlock the Desert (complete the Community Center Boiler Room)

✅ Fall Golden Combo

Recommended: 50% Cranberry (stable continuous harvest) + 30% Pumpkin (high-profit wine) + 20% Grape/Eggplant (for social and foraging bundles). This structure ensures a steady daily cash flow plus the windfall of concentrated wine-making at the end of the month.

Common Fall Pitfalls

  • Under-prepared for Spirit's Eve: The Fall 27 Spirit's Eve festival requires displaying a Pumpkin/Melon. Set aside at least one gold-star specimen in advance.
  • Not clearing the field before frost: All crops wither on Fall 28. Calculate the growth cycle of the last batch carefully to avoid planting right before the season changes.
  • Delaying the Community Center into winter: The Fall Crop Bundle can only be completed in fall—missing it means waiting until year 2.

❄️ Winter — Recovery and Pushing Deep

Winter is a recovery period. Crops cannot be planted, so the focus shifts to socializing, pushing deep into the Mines, expanding processing equipment, and preparing for year two. With no watering burden, it is the best time to push the Mines to floor 120.

Winter Key Goals

  • Complete all Community Center restorations (unlock the Greenhouse and Sewers)
  • Push the Mines to floor 120; obtain the Galaxy Sword and large amounts of Iridium Ore
  • Reach 8 hearts with at least 3 NPCs (eligible to propose)
  • Stockpile large numbers of Kegs and Preserves Jars (prepping ingredients for spring processing)
  • Upgrade to Iridium tools (requires Iridium Ore; about 50,000G in costs)
  • Buy Strawberry seeds in advance for spring of year 2

Common Winter Pitfalls

  • Animals unheated: In winter you must install a Heater in the Coop/Barn (2000G from Robin), or animal mood drops to zero and they produce nothing.
  • Truffle shutdown: Pigs don't go outside in winter and don't produce Truffles. You can accept the downtime, or sell the pigs in advance for cash (not recommended).
  • Failed Mine pushes: Use Winter 1-10 to craft Bombs and Staircases, then push deep from Winter 11 onward—don't try to brute-force it.

🏘️ Community Center Progress Sync

💝 Relationship Building Rhythm

In year one you don't need to max out friendship with every NPC, but it's recommended to lock in 3-5 core targets for steady gifting. Twice a week (the maximum effective amount), prioritize "loved" gifts. Suggested route:

  • Spring: Pick 1-2 to focus on (such as Penny or Abigail); reach 4 hearts by late spring.
  • Summer: Expand to 3-4 NPCs, using summer crops (Hot Pepper for Shane, Melon for Maru) as low-cost gifts.
  • Fall: Push core targets to 8 hearts to unlock romance events; prepare proposal materials (Mermaid's Pendant, bought from the Old Mariner on the beach on a rainy day for 5000G).
  • Winter: Propose and hold the wedding (after expanding the farmhouse), setting up family life for year two.

💡 Important Note

These are reference goals—you can adjust them to your own pace in actual play. Stardew Valley has no "completion deadline"; enjoying the process matters more than hitting the targets. If you don't reach a season's goal, don't stress—year two brings more tools and skills, and going back to fill in gaps is actually easier.
